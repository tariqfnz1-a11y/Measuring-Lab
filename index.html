Measure Lab

Touch, slide and test. Learn how length, mass, capacity, area, volume and speed units work.

Which unit fits?

Measuring means comparing with a standard unit. Pick a unit that gives a number you can picture, usually between 1 and 1000. A coin is 2 mm thick. Saying 0.000002 km is true but unhelpful.

The prefix ladder

The metric system runs on powers of 10. A prefix says how many base units a unit holds: kilo = 1000, hecto = 100, deca = 10, deci = 1/10, centi = 1/100, milli = 1/1000. Moving right to a smaller unit multiplies by 10 each step. Moving left divides by 10.

Area: square units

Area is the flat surface a shape covers, counted in squares. Because both sides change, area units scale by the square of the length scale: 1 cm = 10 mm, so 1 cm² = 100 mm².

Volume: cubic units

Volume is the space a solid takes up, counted in cubes. Cubic units scale by the cube of the length scale. 1 cm³ holds 1 mL, and a cube 10 cm on each side (1 dm³) holds exactly 1 L.

Capacity: how much a container holds

Capacity is how much liquid a container holds. 1 L = 1000 mL. Pour with the slider or tap an everyday amount.

Speed: distance per time

Speed = distance ÷ time. 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s, so 1 km/h = 1/3.6 m/s. Divide by 3.6 to go from km/h to m/s. Multiply by 3.6 to go back. The road below is drawn to scale, 100 m wide.

How long is the strip?

Read both ends. The length is the right reading minus the left reading. Small marks are 1 mm, numbered marks are 1 cm.
